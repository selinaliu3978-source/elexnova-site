Factory Capability and Quality Update for Custom Magnetic Components

Buyers evaluating a magnetic component supplier usually ask the same questions: who actually builds the parts, how large is the production capacity, which quality system is in place, and who answers the engineering questions during sample development. This company update summarizes the current manufacturing and quality position behind MiDEN magnetic components so sourcing teams can compare it against their own supplier requirements.

Manufacturing Base and Production Capacity

MiDEN produces inductors, coils, high frequency transformers and custom magnetic components at two manufacturing bases in Guangzhou and Suining. The current production capacity is approximately 500,000 pieces per day, supported by more than 200 skilled production staff. Capacity at this level matters for two reasons: it keeps trial orders and mass production on the same process, and it gives buyers a realistic view of lead time when demand increases.

Production areas cover automated winding equipment, assembly stations and inspection points. Winding automation is important for repeatability, because inductance tolerance, DCR consistency and interlayer spacing all depend on how consistently the wire is placed. For custom projects, the same equipment can be configured for different core sizes, wire gauges and termination styles without moving the project to a different supplier.

Quality System and Process Control

The manufacturing system is organized around ISO9001:2015 quality management. In practice this means incoming material control, process inspection and outgoing verification are documented rather than handled by memory. Sample confirmation comes first: the approved sample, its drawing and the agreed test items become the reference for production.

Electrical testing is defined by project requirement. Typical checks include inductance, DCR, turns ratio, polarity, insulation resistance and hi-pot, depending on whether the part is an inductor, a common mode choke or a transformer. Visual inspection covers winding appearance, solder joints, marking and mechanical dimensions. Records are kept so that a delivered batch can be traced back to its production and inspection data when a customer requests it.

Engineering Support from Drawing to Sample

Custom magnetic components are usually developed from a customer drawing, a target specification or a sample in hand. Fifteen in-house engineers review the application conditions before quoting: switching frequency, current waveform, saturation margin, temperature rise, size limit, mounting method and insulation requirement.

This review is where supplier response quality is decided. A quote that only repeats a catalog part number is rarely enough. The useful answer explains which core material, wire size and winding structure are recommended, where the thermal risk sits, and which parameters the customer should confirm during sample evaluation. When the target is not achievable in the requested size, the engineering team should say so early rather than after tooling.

OEM and ODM Project Scope

OEM and ODM work at MiDEN covers custom winding, core selection, package and lead forming, marking and project-based packaging. Typical custom projects include power inductors for converters, high frequency transformers for switch-mode supplies, common mode chokes for EMI filter stages, toroidal and drum core inductors for industrial equipment, and planar magnetics for compact designs.

Because the same factory produces both catalog families and custom designs, a customer who starts with a standard series can later move to a customized version without changing supplier, documentation or quality process. That continuity reduces re-qualification effort during a product revision.

What to Include in an RFQ

To receive a usable technical response and quotation, an RFQ should include the application, target inductance or turns ratio, rated and peak current, operating frequency, voltage and insulation requirement, maximum dimensions, mounting and termination style, ambient temperature, target price level and expected annual volume. Sample or drawing files, existing test data and compliance requirements such as RoHS or REACH should be attached when available.

Next Steps for Supplier Evaluation

Sourcing teams that need evidence before adding a supplier can request production capability information, sample lead time, quality documents and reference specifications. A short technical call is usually the fastest way to confirm whether a specific project fits the available process, and sample evaluation then confirms the decision with measured results.
